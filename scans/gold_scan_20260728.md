## Background note — reading the OI-change screen

Context carried in from the same session, on how to interpret the CME OI-change grid
(GCQ6 4042.1, strikes 4025 / 4050 / 4080 / 4100 / 4125):

- **OI change counts contracts, not direction.** Every contract has a buyer *and* a seller.
  `+160` at 4080 does not mean 160 calls were bought.
- **Sign meaning:** positive = new positions opened (fresh conviction at that strike);
  negative (e.g. 4050 C −106, 4100 C −123) = positions closed, that level is losing relevance.
- **Who holds the "price won't get there" view is the seller.** For a 0DTE strike 38 points
  OTM, the base case is premium *selling*, not buying.
- **Telling buyers from sellers:** (1) OI up + premium up = aggressive buyers; OI up +
  premium down = sellers hitting bids. (2) Distance from spot. (3) Volume ≫ OI change =
  day-traded flat, not real positioning.
- **Why walls become levels:** dealers hedge in futures. Long-gamma dealers sell into rallies
  at the call wall (resistance) and buy dips at the put wall (support). If dealers are *short*
  gamma, the same walls flip from brakes to accelerants — which is why a call wall that breaks
  tends to break violently.
- **CME publishes OI after settlement, next business day.** So "OI Change" is a map to draw
  before the open, not a live intraday flow indicator.
